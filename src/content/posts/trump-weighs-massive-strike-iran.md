## Trump Weighs 'Massive' Strike on Iran After 13 Nights of Military Operations

After 13 consecutive nights of U.S. military strikes against targets inside Iran, the first pause came overnight into Saturday, July 25 — but President Trump made clear the restraint may be temporary.

### What's Happening

Speaking to reporters Friday, the President stated he has not made a final decision on whether to dramatically expand the military campaign, saying the U.S. remains "locked and loaded" while diplomatic channels stay open.

> "They really shouldn't test the will of the United States, at least not under President Donald Trump." — Secretary of State Marco Rubio

Secretary Rubio, filling in as White House press secretary, outlined two paths for Tehran: a diplomatic route leading to "reconstruction, prosperity, and stability" — or "growing isolation, economic collapse, and ultimately total defeat."

### Why It Matters

The conflict centers on Iran's attempts to restrict navigation through the **Strait of Hormuz**, one of the world's most critical oil chokepoints. The disruption has sent **Brent crude prices surging above $100 per barrel** and contributed to rising gas prices at home — a kitchen-table issue hitting families across Bastrop County hard.

Meanwhile, Iran-backed **Houthi rebels** in Yemen have escalated their own campaign, attacking Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea and claiming a missile strike on the Saudi city of Jizan.

### The Bottom Line

President Trump's strategy combines overwhelming military capability with diplomatic openings — demonstrating that American strength and willingness to negotiate are not mutually exclusive. The coming days will be critical in determining whether Iran chooses the deal or the consequences.
