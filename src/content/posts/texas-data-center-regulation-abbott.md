## Texas Data Center Debate: Abbott Pushes Regulation as Grid Concerns Mount

Governor Greg Abbott has directed state regulators — the Public Utilities Commission of Texas (PUC) and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) — to establish new rules governing data center operations, responding to growing concerns about the impact of massive AI and cloud computing facilities on the Texas power grid.

### The Issue

Texas has become one of the nation's top destinations for data centers, driven by cheap energy, available land, and a business-friendly regulatory environment. But the explosive growth of AI computing has placed enormous new demands on the state's electrical grid.

Key concerns include:

- **Massive energy consumption** — a single large data center can use as much electricity as a small city
- **Grid reliability** — Texas experienced deadly grid failures during Winter Storm Uri in 2021, and leaders want to prevent future crises
- **Competition for resources** — data centers competing with residential customers for limited power supply

### Abbott's Solution

Rather than imposing a blanket moratorium (as some Democrats have proposed), Governor Abbott is taking a **market-friendly regulatory approach**:

- New PUC rules will require data centers to demonstrate grid impact assessments before connecting
- ERCOT will have enhanced oversight of large-load connections
- The state will prioritize grid reliability while maintaining Texas' competitive advantage

### Why It Matters

This is a distinctly **Republican approach** to a complex problem: using smart regulation to protect consumers and grid reliability without killing the economic opportunity that data centers represent. Texas attracted billions in tech investment precisely because of its pro-business environment — and Abbott is working to keep it that way.
